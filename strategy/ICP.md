# ICP and question bank

## The ICP

B2B software companies, 20–200 people, selling a product that buyers compare
before they buy, with a named competitor set and an existing content function —
even if that function is one person.

The buyer is the founder, or a head of growth or SEO who already owns
comparison and alternative pages. Their trigger is usually a moment of
disbelief: they asked an AI tool about their own category and watched it
recommend three competitors without them.

## Why this ICP rather than a broader one

They already do the work the product recommends. A team that has never written
a comparison page will receive a brief and do nothing with it, and the
before/after will never materialise. A team with an existing content motion can
ship a brief in a week, which is the only way a 90-day window produces a result.

They also have the instrumentation. GA4 and a CDN in front of a modern stack
means Signal B is a configuration change rather than an engineering project.

## Start with one vertical

Project management and adjacent work-management tools. This is deliberate: the
sample workspace already models Acme against Asana, Notion, ClickUp and Monday,
the comparison vocabulary is well developed, and buyers ask "best X for Y" and
"alternative to Z" questions constantly. The demo data, the question bank and
the outreach conversations all describe the same world.

Expand to developer tools and analytics platforms only after the first vertical
produces a documented before/after. Two half-covered verticals are worse than
one that is convincing.

## Disqualifiers

- No named competitors, or a category where the buyer never compares options.
- No one who owns content. The brief will land on nobody.
- Enterprise procurement as the first motion. Log access and pilot agreements
  will outlast the quarter.
- Anyone whose interest is "watching the space" rather than winning a specific
  question. Enthusiasm without a question to win produces nothing to measure.

## Question bank: 28 questions for project management

Intent stages are not decoration. Discovery questions accumulate volume,
comparison questions carry the decision, and decision questions carry revenue.
Report each stage separately.

### Discovery (10)

1. What's the best project management tool for a small team?
2. What's the best project management software for a startup?
3. Which project management tools work well for remote teams?
4. What's a good tool for managing tasks across a small company?
5. Which project management apps are easy to set up?
6. What do teams use to track projects and deadlines?
7. What's the best free project management tool?
8. Which project management tool is best for a team of 20?
9. What's the best tool for a founder managing a small team?
10. Which tools combine tasks, docs, and team chat?

### Comparison (12)

11. What's the best Asana alternative for project tracking?
12. What's the best Notion alternative for a product team?
13. Is Monday better than Asana for engineering teams?
14. What's a simpler alternative to Asana?
15. Which project management tool is closest to ClickUp but simpler?
16. What's the best alternative to Trello for growing teams?
17. Should a small team use Asana or Notion?
18. What's the best project management tool that isn't Jira?
19. Which tools are similar to Linear but for non-engineering teams?
20. What's the best Asana alternative for agencies?
21. Which project management tool has the best comparison to Monday?
22. What do teams switch to when Asana gets too complex?

### Decision (6)

23. How much does [brand] cost for a team of 20?
24. Is [brand] worth the price for a small team?
25. Which project management tool has the best free plan?
26. What's the best project management tool for a company with contract staff?
27. Can you migrate from Asana to another tool without losing data?
28. Which project management tool has the best support?

## Question hygiene

- **Brand-neutral phrasing.** "What's the best tool for X" measures the category.
  "Is [brand] good" measures reputation. Both are useful; keep them in separate
  sets and never blend them into one share.
- **One question, one decision.** "What's the best cheap tool for a small remote
  agency" is four questions wearing a coat. Split it.
- **Twenty-five to thirty per partner, not more.** Coverage matters less than a
  set the partner has read and agreed with. If they dispute the question set,
  the whole report is disputable.
- **The control set comes from the same stages.** A control made only of
  discovery questions will not be comparable to a treated set of decision
  questions.
- **Revisit quarterly.** Questions age, and a question nobody asks anymore
  should leave the set rather than dilute it.
